CAPSTONE · AUG 2026–PRESENT · PROJECT MANAGER

Helmet moisture extraction system

A portable drying station for full-face helmets, developed by Team APEX at the University of Houston. The design balances moisture removal with power, noise, size, and compatibility.

Project approach & current status

I manage scope, assignments, milestones, and team coordination. Our approach compares passive drying against directed airflow using repeatable moisture loading and mass measurements.

The progress-report draft records completed passive baseline testing and more than 20 helmet measurements. Concept selection, fabrication, and prototype validation remain development work; drying-time targets are not yet demonstrated results.

Design considerations include reaching crown, cheek, and rear padding, supporting different helmet sizes, and avoiding permanent helmet modifications.

Thermal / fluids · Experimental design · Project management

MECHANICAL DESIGN I · SEP–DEC 2024 · MECHANICAL DESIGNER

One gripper. Three object geometries.

A remote-controlled mobile robot designed to pick up spheres, cones, and cylinders and deposit them into containers up to 10 inches high.

My contribution & design decisions

In a four-person team, I led concept development and created the complete SolidWorks model: parts, assemblies, mates, and drawings. I sized drive motors using torque and gear-ratio calculations and co-developed Arduino motor-control code.

A wide-claw gripper handled all three shapes. Omnidirectional wheels and a rear caster supported tight turns; FDM structural parts helped control mass and speed up revisions.

Across four build-and-test revisions, we integrated the mechanical and electrical systems. The robot met the project’s size, weight, and object-handling requirements.

SolidWorks · FDM printing · Arduino · Motor sizing

HARRIS COUNTY · FALL 2025 · LEAD INTERN

Parking garage LED retrofit

A $135,000 retrofit replacing more than 300 fluorescent fixtures and reducing connected lighting load by approximately 50%.

Engineering decisions & delivery

I evaluated fixtures against illuminance, compatibility, cost, and controls, selecting selectable-wattage LED canopy fixtures with motion sensing and Bluetooth group zoning.

I redesigned mounting to attach directly to junction boxes, clearing security-camera sightlines and improving maintenance access. AutoCAD layouts, phased installation planning, field verification, and CenterPoint Energy incentive documentation supported delivery.

The reported reduction refers to connected lighting load, not a measured annual energy-bill reduction.

ENGINEERING PRACTICUM · SEP–DEC 2024 · TEAM PROJECT

A press fit that carries its load

Designed an elephant figure and acrylic base whose interference fit supported a 500 g cantilevered load using friction alone.

Design, fabrication & verification

I modeled the mating parts in SolidWorks, applied GD&T and tolerance analysis, CNC-milled the acrylic base, and verified assembly fit and load capacity.

The interface needed enough interference to retain the load while still allowing hand assembly without cracking the acrylic. The project connected drawing tolerances to fabrication capability and physical testing.

GD&T · Tolerance analysis · CNC machining · Load testing
